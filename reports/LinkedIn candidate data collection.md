# Ask candidates for LinkedIn data, never scrape it

**HireFlow should not fetch a candidate's LinkedIn page from the URL on their resume. There is no official LinkedIn API that turns a pasted profile URL into profile data, and every other way of doing it (your own scraper, a headless browser, a scraping API) breaches LinkedIn's User Agreement.** The sanctioned route to a member's profile is the member acting themselves. For HireFlow that means the candidate uploads LinkedIn's "Save to PDF" file or the "Get a copy of your data" export, which HireFlow parses with its existing resume pipeline. "Sign in with LinkedIn" (OpenID Connect) is open to every developer but returns only name, photo and an optional email, so it works as a login and identity-confirmation step, not as enrichment. Full-profile APIs are either partner-gated (Recruiter System Connect, Apply Connect) or limited to EEA and Swiss members (Member Data Portability), and none suits a young Indian startup today. Third-party "LinkedIn URL in, profile out" vendors are the highest-risk option: LinkedIn sued and shut down Proxycurl in 2025 and sued ProAPIs in October 2025. This report is research, not legal advice, and several points rest on secondary sources, which are flagged.

## LinkedIn's contract bans scraping even when the profile is public

The legal picture splits into two layers that people often conflate: whether scraping is a crime, and whether it breaches LinkedIn's contract. On the first, the Ninth Circuit held in 2022 that scraping publicly accessible pages is not access "without authorization" under the CFAA, and that violating a site's terms alone is not CFAA liability ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2022/12/linkedin-v-hiq-landmark-data-scraping-suit-provides-guidance-to-data-scrapers-and-web-operators)). On the second, the same litigation went badly for the scraper: the district court held that the User Agreement's prohibitions on scraping and fake profiles are enforceable, and hiQ ended with a stipulated **$500,000 judgment, a permanent injunction and deletion of its data and algorithms** (same source). The consent judgment has no precedential value, and hiQ is US law that Indian courts do not follow, but it shows what LinkedIn does when it wins on contract.

The contract text is unambiguous. Section 8.2 of the User Agreement (effective 3 November 2025) bars "software, scripts, robots or any other means" to scrape or copy profiles, bots and unauthorised automated access, and circumventing access controls or use limits ([LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)). The "Prohibited software and extensions" help page extends this to third-party crawlers, plug-ins and automation tools ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387)). A server-side HTTP fetch, headless browser or proxy request for a resume URL falls inside these rules whether or not the page is publicly viewable. Whether a logged-out, non-member fetch is bound by the contract at all is contested, so the exposure is less certain than for a logged-in scraper, but that uncertainty is not something a product should be built on.

Candidate consent changes the data-protection position but not LinkedIn's contract. Section 8.2 has no carve-out for scraping done on the member's behalf, and the Proxycurl-style complaints (fake accounts, circumvention) were not cured by the profile owners' wishes. That second point is the report's inference from the complaint allegations, not a ruling ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-wins-legal-case-data-scrapers-proxycurl/756101/)). If HireFlow only stores the URL as a reference link and never fetches it, exposure under LinkedIn's terms is minimal, though the URL is still personal data.

Data-protection law adds a second constraint. Under GDPR, public availability does not remove the duty of notice and a lawful basis: the French regulator fined Kaspr **EUR 240,000** in January 2025 for collecting contact data, including from LinkedIn, without informing people or establishing a valid legal basis (secondary sources, the decision itself was not read) ([Compliance Hub](https://compliancehub.wiki/understanding-the-french-supervisory-authoritys-eu240-000-fine-against-kaspr-for-data-scraping/)). In India, the DPDP Act excludes personal data that the individual herself made publicly available (s.3(c)(ii)), but the test is how it became public, and the exclusion does not resolve website terms or computer-access law ([India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-dpdp-act-publicly-available-personal-data-46899.html/)). The commencement dates are reported inconsistently: the Rules were notified around 13 November 2025, and one source puts the main substantive duties at 13 May 2027 ([EY](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/alerts-hub/2025/11/digital-data-protection-act-rules-notified-by-meity.pdf); [HLC](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023-brought-into-force-)). Candidate-supplied data with a clear notice satisfies all of these regimes more easily than third-party sourcing, and the DPDP exclusion should be confirmed with Indian counsel.

## The official routes are real but none returns a profile for a pasted URL

The notes reviewed five official or semi-official routes, and the pattern is consistent: openness and data richness trade off against each other.

| Route | Who can use it | What it returns | Fit for HireFlow |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sign In with LinkedIn (OIDC) | Any developer, self-serve | `sub`, name, picture, locale, optional email | Login and prefill only |
| Member Data Portability, 3rd Party | Verified businesses; EEA and Swiss members only | Profile, positions, education, skills and more | Only if serving EU candidates |
| Recruiter System Connect, Apply Connect, Apply with LinkedIn, SNAP | Approved partners; Apply with LinkedIn closed to new partners | Candidate sync, applicants of jobs posted through the integration, "matched" public profiles (SNAP) | Not realistic near term |
| Candidate "Save to PDF" or data archive | Every member, no developer approval | Resume-style PDF, or CSV archive | Best fit |

Sign In with LinkedIn requests the `openid`, `profile` and `email` scopes and the `/v2/userinfo` endpoint returns name, picture and an optional email; Microsoft's documentation states it "does not verify user identities and should not be marketed as such" ([MS Learn: Sign In with LinkedIn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/consumer/integrations/self-serve/sign-in-with-linkedin-v2)). Fuller profile fields (positions, education, skills) are available only to approved partner programs ([MS Learn: Profile Fields](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/shared/references/v2/profile)). The API Terms add two constraints that matter for an assessment product: content may not be used to determine eligibility for employment, and storing profile data requires the member's legally valid consent. The self-serve program is also capped at roughly 100,000 lifetime users ([LinkedIn API Terms](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/l/api-terms-of-use)). Whether scoring a candidate's assessment alongside API-sourced profile data counts as an "employment eligibility" use needs to be put to LinkedIn directly.

Member Data Portability is the only official API returning rich profile domains (PROFILE, POSITIONS, EDUCATION, SKILLS and others) with the member's consent, but it exists to satisfy the Digital Markets Act and **only EEA and Swiss members can consent**. The 3rd Party variant requires a verified Company Page, a business-verification review and a LinkedIn-reviewed access request ([MS Learn: MDP 3rd Party](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/dma/member-data-portability/member-data-portability-3rd-party/)). It is irrelevant to Indian candidates and worth revisiting only if HireFlow sells to EU employers. If it does, the `domain` parameter allows requesting just the profile-type domains, which is the data-minimal choice ([Member Snapshot API](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/dma/member-data-portability/shared/member-snapshot-api)).

The partner programs are aimed at established ATS vendors. The Talent partner application asks for customer regions, number of customers, the five largest customers and average daily active jobs, and LinkedIn prioritises integrations that serve existing Recruiter and Jobs customers, with no guaranteed agreement and no published timeline ([LinkedIn Talent partner application](https://business.linkedin.com/talent-solutions/ats-partners/partner-application)). Apply Connect requires customers to hold a Recruiter license, and "Apply with LinkedIn" states it is not accepting new partners ([MS Learn: Apply with LinkedIn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/talent/apply-with-linkedin/apply-with-linkedin)). Even for approved partners, data arrives only for applicants to jobs posted through the integration, not for arbitrary candidates. The notes found no explicit official sentence saying URL lookup is not offered; the conclusion rests on the absence of any such capability in the documentation and the partner gating.

Candidate-initiated exports fill the gap. LinkedIn's archive ("Get a copy of your data") is available to every member and delivered by emailed link ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a566336)). Save to PDF is desktop-only, English-profile-only, and capped at **200 downloads per month** per member according to LinkedIn's help page ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a541960)); a third-party guide quotes 100, so rely on the official figure and re-check it. Targeted exports are reported ready in about ten minutes and the full archive in up to 24 hours (third-party reports, not confirmed on the help page).

## Third-party providers carry the legal risk you are trying to avoid

LinkedIn has shown it will litigate against data vendors, and the survivors are hard to verify. Proxycurl shut down around July 2025 after LinkedIn's January 2025 suit, with a court-entered permanent injunction requiring it to delete unauthorised LinkedIn data and **send the injunction to its customers** ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-wins-legal-case-data-scrapers-proxycurl/756101/)). The founder said about half of Proxycurl's revenue came from scraping LinkedIn ([Nubela](https://nubela.co/blog/what-is-proxycurl-api-now-in-2026-im-the-founder/)). In October 2025 LinkedIn sued ProAPIs, Netswift and the founder in N.D. California over alleged fake-account scraping resold for up to $15,000 a month; an "agreement in principle" was reported in February 2026 with no final disposition found ([Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/183001/security/linkedin-sues-proapis-for-15k-month-linkedin-data-scraping-scheme.html)). An earlier precedent is HiringSolved, which paid $40,000 and destroyed scraped data in 2014 ([Winston & Strawn](https://www.winston.com/en/blogs-and-podcasts/privacy-law-corner/company-pays-40k-settlement-for-allegedly-scraping-linkedin-corp)). Meta's and X's losses against Bright Data concerned logged-out public data on other platforms, not LinkedIn ([Farella Braun](https://www.fbm.com/technology/publications/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-and-online-data-collection-meta-platforms-v-bright-data/)).

Cost is not the barrier. Coresignal's documented plans run from $49 for 2,500 credits to $5,000 for 10 million, with a core employee record costing 10 credits, which works out to roughly **$0.02 to $0.08 per record** at Starter to Pro tiers (the report's arithmetic from [Coresignal pricing](https://docs.coresignal.com/pricing)). People Data Labs is reported at about $0.20 to $0.28 per successful match, and RapidAPI-style live scrapers at $0.005 to $0.05 per request, from reseller and review sites rather than vendor pricing pages ([FullEnrich](https://fullenrich.com/content/people-data-labs-pricing); [Cotera](https://cotera.co/articles/linkedin-scraping-tools-comparison.md)). Apollo and RocketReach are mainly contact databases and not live-profile pulls.

The risks are structural. Any vendor whose product is "LinkedIn URL in, live profile out" is the same business as Proxycurl and ProAPIs. Customers face the vendor vanishing overnight, orders to purge data, and an inability to show candidates or regulators where the data came from. The notes found no case of LinkedIn suing a vendor's customer, so direct liability is unquantified rather than disproven. Under GDPR, data bought from a third party triggers Art. 14 notice duties, and the Kaspr fine shows regulators act on LinkedIn-sourced data. Foreign vendors also raise cross-border transfer and processor-agreement questions that were not researched. The notes could not verify the 2026 operating status, sourcing statements, output schemas or deletion mechanisms for PDL, Apollo, RocketReach, Bright Data or RapidAPI providers from primary sources. If enrichment is ever essential, the only defensible option is a licensed vendor with contractual sourcing warranties and deletion support, never a live scraper.

## Ranked recommendation: candidate-supplied data first, vendors last

The ranking below orders options by legal risk, effort and value for an Indian assessment SaaS.

| Rank | Option | Risk | Effort | Value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Candidate uploads LinkedIn PDF or data-export ZIP | Lowest | Low | High: structured work history, education, skills |
| 2 | Resume link extraction, with candidate-confirmed URL stored as reference only | Minimal | Low | Medium: GitHub and portfolio enrichment via public APIs |
| 3 | Sign in with LinkedIn (OIDC) | Low | Low | Low: name, email, photo, proof of account control |
| 4 | Member Data Portability 3rd Party (EU customers only) | Low, but approval-gated | High | High for EEA members |
| 5 | Licensed data vendor with sourcing warranties | Medium | Medium | Medium; needs DPDP/GDPR diligence |
| 6 | Partner programs (RSC, Apply Connect) | Low once approved | Very high | High, but only for an established ATS |
| Avoid | Own scraper, headless browser, scraping APIs | High | Medium | Not recommended |

Ranks 1 and 2 deliver most of the achievable value at almost no legal risk and reuse infrastructure HireFlow already has. Rank 3 adds a convenience and a weak identity signal, not content. A self-supplied PDF is still self-reported and can be edited, so it counts as another candidate claim, not verification. That framing matters for fairness: the consistency check should present flags to a human reviewer, not a score or rejection signal.

## Implementation outline for the FastAPI backend

**Step one is link extraction at resume upload.** Run two passes and deduplicate. First read real hyperlink annotations with PyMuPDF `page.get_links()`, keeping entries that carry a `uri` key, or pdfplumber `page.hyperlinks` ([PyMuPDF tutorial](https://pymupdftest.readthedocs.io/en/stable/tutorial.html); [pdfplumber README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/jsvine/pdfplumber@stable/README.md)); resumes often show an icon or the word "LinkedIn" whose target exists only in the annotation. Second, run a regex over the extracted text for `linkedin.com/in/<slug>`, `github.com/<user>`, `stackoverflow.com/users/<id>` and other http(s) URLs, stripping trailing punctuation and tracking parameters. For DOCX, scan hyperlink relationships and `w:hyperlink` elements in body, headers and footers (background knowledge, untested). PyMuPDF is AGPL or commercial licensed, which matters for a commercial product and was not researched, so check the license before relying on it. Image-only resumes have no annotations and need OCR, which was also not researched.

**Step two is candidate confirmation, which doubles as the consent point.** In the invite or onboarding flow, show the extracted links, let the candidate edit or remove them, and present a standalone itemised notice stating what will be read, for what purpose, how long it is kept, and that refusing does not affect their assessment. Store the consent record with a timestamp and notice version. This matches the DPDP requirement for specific, informed consent with easy withdrawal and the GDPR preference for an explicit opt-in for EU candidates. Never fetch `linkedin.com` server-side; store the confirmed URL as a plain reference field.

**Step three is the LinkedIn content ingestion.** Offer two upload paths with short in-product instructions. The PDF path reuses the existing resume parser: extract text with PyMuPDF, then use an LLM or heuristics anchored on LinkedIn's fixed headings (Experience, Education, Skills, Summary, Certifications), because the sidebar contact column can scramble reading order. The layout detail is unverified, so test against real exports, and expect non-English profiles to be unsupported. The ZIP path should read only the Profile, Positions, Education and Skills CSVs, matching filenames loosely since LinkedIn changes them, and discard every other file in the archive immediately for data minimisation. Delete the uploaded originals after parsing and keep only the structured fields for the stated retention period. Optionally add Sign in with LinkedIn through `/v2/userinfo` for name, email and picture, treating the result as account control, not identity verification, and keep the picture out of any scoring or LLM input.

**Step four is optional enrichment from other public sources, using the candidate-confirmed handles.** For GitHub, hold one app-level token in the backend and fetch the user profile, 5 to 10 recently pushed non-fork repos and per-repo languages, roughly 12 to 15 calls against a 5,000-per-hour authenticated limit (unauthenticated is 60 per hour) ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/using-the-rest-api/rate-limits-for-the-rest-api)). Cache per candidate and label bytes-of-code as a weak signal. For Stack Overflow, use a registered API key to lift the reported 300-per-day unauthenticated limit, and treat reputation as weakly related to skill. For portfolio pages, fetch only the supplied URL, respect robots.txt, identify the User-Agent, cap size and time, block private and loopback address ranges to prevent SSRF, extract text only, and treat the content as untrusted input to avoid prompt injection. Kaggle was not researched.

**Step five is the cross-check shown to HR.** Give the LLM the resume text and the supplied profile text and require JSON output where every finding carries the claim, a verbatim quote from each source, a type (match, not found, differs, ambiguous) and a confidence; drop any finding without quotes. Use the wording "not found in supplied profile" or "needs clarification" with a neutral interview question, never "false". Do not compute a single trust score, keep the check out of automated ranking (or audit it for disparate impact), and exclude photos and age or gender proxies such as graduation dates from the input. Show candidates what was compared and let them correct or explain. This design anticipates EU AI Act high-risk obligations for recruitment tools (stated from background knowledge, not verified on EUR-Lex) and New York City Local Law 144, which requires an annual bias audit and notice for automated employment decision tools ([CASRAI](https://casrai.org/wp/?p=1470)).

## Conclusion

The question as posed, "collect LinkedIn data from the link on the resume", has no compliant technical answer, and the research reframes it: the resume link is best treated as a pointer to ask the candidate for a file, not as an address to fetch. The strongest signal in the evidence is that LinkedIn enforces its contract against scrapers and their vendors rather than against the CFAA question that scrapers like to cite, so HireFlow's real exposure from a vendor-based shortcut is losing the data supply, being forced to purge it, and being unable to show candidates its provenance.

Three items remain open and are worth resolving before launch. Confirm with Indian counsel that candidate-supplied files fit the DPDP notice and consent design once the main provisions commence, and confirm the exact commencement dates, which the sources report inconsistently. Ask LinkedIn whether showing profile-derived data next to an assessment result triggers the API Terms ban on using content for employment eligibility, which only matters if HireFlow adopts the OIDC or Member Data Portability routes. Finally, test the PDF and CSV parsers against real exports, since field reliability in the notes is an inference and no sample file was inspected.
